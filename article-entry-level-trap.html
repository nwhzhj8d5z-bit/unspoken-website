The entry-level trap

By You · Issue 01 · 6 min read

Three months ago, I graduated with a double degree. I had a plan: apply, interview, get hired, start building a career. I did not plan for the part where "entry-level" would turn out to be a lie.

Every job I'm qualified for on paper wants three-plus years of experience I don't have. Every internship I'm qualified for by experience wants a current student ID I don't have either. I graduated into a gap that doesn't have a name on any job posting, but I live in it every single day.

"We were promised a ladder. We got a locked door."

My mom doesn't believe me. Not exactly — she believes I'm trying. She just doesn't believe the door is locked. She thinks I'm not knocking hard enough. "Just go in person," she tells me. "Ask nicely. Shake someone's hand. That's how I got my first job."
